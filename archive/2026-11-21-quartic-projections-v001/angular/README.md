# A phase measurement that identifies the quartic branch

At norm 73, my two conjugate quartic branches give opposite angular coefficients in the ordered Gaussian frame. I wanted a measurement that uses the signs at every norm. At the smoothing parameter $t=1/\sqrt D$, I obtain a nonzero angular theta value whose argument is $-0.401905193375097$ radians to within $1.2\times10^{-14}$. The conjugate quartic has the opposite angle. The Fourier transform predicts both directions before the sum is evaluated. ## From the three signs to the two directions

I use the ordered frame $z=a+bi$, the discriminant $\delta=A+Bi=-5045155+1027708i$, and $D=|\delta|^2=26509772707289$. Write $r=-A/B\bmod D=11825711960877$. The three original residue symbols multiply to the Jacobi symbol

$$
f(a,b)=\left(\frac{a+rb}{D}\right).
$$

The fourth power in the angular kernel $H$ keeps its direction when the generator is multiplied by a Gaussian unit. The sign is unit-invariant too, since $r^2=-1\bmod D$ and $r$ is a quadratic residue at every prime dividing $D$. The factor of four counts each ideal once.

$$
H(t)=\frac14\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb Z}
 f(a,b)(a+bi)^4e^{-\pi t(a^2+b^2)}.
$$

For additive frequency $(h,k)$, substitute $u=a+rb$ in the finite Fourier transform. Summing over $b$ gives zero unless $k=rh\bmod D$. On that line the remaining sum is the primitive quadratic Gauss sum, giving $D\sqrt D\,(h/D)$, with $(h/D)$ the Jacobi symbol. The frequency lattice is generated by $(A,B)$ and $(-B,A)$. The two vectors satisfy the congruence and their determinant is $D$, its index. Thus $h+ik=\delta(u+iv)$, and the dual sign is $(h/D)=f(u,-v)$ because $Ar=B\bmod D$ and $(A/D)=1$. [Montgomery and Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I*, Chapter 9](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511618314.011) · [Finite Fourier identities in Lean](../QuarticDualLine.lean)

With Fourier kernel $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}$, the transform of $z^4e^{-\pi t|z|^2}$ is $t^{-5}\xi^4e^{-\pi|\xi|^2/t}$. Poisson summation on residue classes uses the frequencies $(h+ik)/D$. Substitution of the dual lattice and $v\mapsto-v$ gives

$$
H(t)=\frac{\delta^4}{D^{9/2}t^5}
\overline{H\!\left(\frac1{Dt}\right)}.
$$

At $t=1/\sqrt D$ the two smoothing parameters agree. Hence $H=(\delta^4/D^2)\bar H$. Each nonzero value lies on the real line through $\delta^2$ in the complex output plane, with exact slope

$$
\frac{2AB}{A^2-B^2}=-\frac{10369892309480}{24397405240761}.
$$

Conjugating the quartic reverses that slope. A zero reading is the single point shared by both lines. The angle is measured modulo $\pi$, which allows either sign along the chosen line.

For reproducibility, put $M=9000$, $c=\pi/\sqrt D$, and $Q=2^{80}$. The program encloses each $e^{-cj^2}$ with Arb and proves the integer value of $\lfloor Qe^{-cj^2}\rfloor$. It then sums the signed quartic polynomials times the two integer weights exactly, using 45-bit pieces bounded before multiplication. Replacing both one-coordinate weights costs at most $8M^5(M+1)/Q<3.518$ in each component. The outer lattice shell $\max(a,b)=k$ has $2k$ representatives. $$

=\frac{4e^{-v}(v^2+2v+2)}{c^3}<15.378,\qquad v=cM^2.
$$

## Projection onto the critical line

Let $W_\eta(s)=L_{\mathbb Q(i)}(s,\eta\omega_4)$, with $\omega_4((z))=(z/|z|)^4$. Its Mellin kernel is $H(t)t^{s+2}\,dt/t$. With completion $\Lambda_W(s)=(\sqrt D/\pi)^s\Gamma(s+2)W_\eta(s)$, the substitution $t\mapsto1/(Dt)$ in that integral gives

$$
\Lambda_W(s)=\frac{\delta^4}{D^2}\,
\overline{\Lambda_W(1-\bar s)}.
$$

At $s=1/2+iT$, both arguments of $\Lambda_W$ are the same. Therefore **every nonzero completed angular reading has the same slope just measured**, at every height, in the stated normalization and frame. At the Dottie height this predicts the branch line conditional on a nonzero reading. The direct measurement above evaluates the heat kernel $H$ at its self-dual scale. A critical-line value of $W_\eta$ weights the entire range of smoothing scales in its Mellin integral. For the scalar Gaussian factor the fourth angular power is absent, so reflection gives multiplier $+1$ on both branches. Excluding a zero from its Dottie disks still requires the independent scalar remainder estimate. ## Doubling the angular power

The same calculation makes a prediction for each integer $j\ge1$. Replace the fourth power in $H$ by $z^{4j}$ and the angular character in $W_\eta$ by $\omega_4^j$. The Gaussian transform contributes $(-i)^{4j}=1$, and writing the frequency as $\delta(u+iv)/D$ contributes $\delta^{4j}$. At the self-dual scale the root number is $(\delta/\sqrt D)^{4j}$. The argument of a nonzero reading is consequently $2j\arg\delta\bmod\pi$; conjugating the quartic changes its sign. Mellin completion now uses $\Gamma(s+2j)$ and gives the same two lines for critical-line readings.

For every positive $j$ these two branch lines are distinct. Coincidence would give $4j\arg\delta\in\pi\mathbb Z$, making $\delta/\bar\delta$ a root of unity in $\mathbb Q(i)$. The only such roots are $1,-1,i,-i$. Both coordinates of $\delta/\bar\delta=(A^2-B^2+2ABi)/D$ are nonzero. Thus the measured phase can distinguish the branches at every positive angular order divisible by four, whenever the reading is nonzero.

For $j=2$, the prediction fixed before evaluation was $-0.8038103867501943$ radians on the chosen branch. The coordinate-wise weight approximation and tail bound use the same proof with $|z|^8\le16k^8$ on shell $k$.

The measured real component is $(6.443769503664563\pm0.0000000000514)\times10^{28}$. The imaginary component is $(-6.685536540104979\pm0.0000000000514)\times10^{28}$. Their argument is **$-0.8038103867501943247\pm7.82\times10^{-12}$ radians**, which selects the predicted branch at angular order eight.
