    cos θ = 2(H− − H+),        sin θ = 2(1 − H+ − H−)/sqrt(3).

3. The arithmetic family and the angle

The comparison with the cosine reference gives π^(−w) times the bilateral
Hurwitz zeta Z(w,b)=ζ(w,b)+ζ(w,1−b), where w=2s and
b={1/2−θ/π}. At the source parameters, reflection interchanges b and 1−b
and hence leaves this arithmetic function identical for every w.
At the first Riemann zero its magnitude is 2.21428086818 on both starting
flanks, 1.75980638204 at the turned left endpoint, and 0.759244165779 at
the turned right endpoint.

The right turn crosses +60° and +45°. At those arithmetic angles b=1/6
and b=1/4. The same calculation at +30° gives b=1/3. The two unit
residues mod q are +1 and −1 in all three cases. Summing the Dirichlet
series over those residues and continuing analytically gives, away from
the pole w=1,

    R(θ,w)=(q/π)^w ζ(w) product over p|q of (1−p^(−w)).

In particular the endpoints carry the Riemann zeros with their original
multiplicities in Re w>0. Differentiating the Hurwitz factors with
respect to the angle in radians gives

    ∂θ R(θq,w) = w (q/π)^(w+1) L(w+1,χq).

Here χq is +1 on residue 1, −1 on residue −1, and 0 on nonunits. The
right side is nonzero for Re w>0 away from the pole by the Euler product
of L. Holding any nontrivial Riemann zero w fixed therefore gives a
simple zero as a function of angle at each of these special angles.
The sign of the derivative reverses at the reflected angles. These
identities apply to every zero in the open strip. The published strict
zero-free bound transfers at each arithmetic anchor via the nonzero
Euler factors.

At the interior angle +54°, b=1/5. The indicator of the residues ±1
modulo 5 equals (χ₀+χ₅)/2, where χ₀ is the principal character and χ₅
is the quadratic character. On summing the Dirichlet series and
continuing in w this gives

    R(54°,w)=(5/π)^w [(1−5^(−w)) ζ(w) + L(w,χ₅)] / 2.

Thus a zero on an intermediate path can result from cancellation of two
nonzero arithmetic terms. At the zero continued from 4πi/log(3), the
ordinary term is about 0.844286291−0.071301339i and the quadratic term
is its negative. The magnitude of ordinary zeta there is 1.3049615252.
At the other branch's 54-degree zero it is 1.2821801765. Fifth_residue.py
uses the certified checkpoint immediately before b=1/5 on each branch,
joins it to b=1/5, and evaluates both arithmetic terms at the enclosed zero.

The left turn crosses −90°. Here the complementary frequency passes
through zero. For δ=θ+π/2 measured in radians, remove that single mode
|δ|^(−w) from the reference. The remaining sum has a regular even Taylor
expansion, for fixed w≠1,

    2π^(−w)ζ(w) + π^(−w−2) w(w+1)ζ(w+2) δ² + O(δ⁴).

This follows by shifting the Hurwitz parameters to 1±|δ|/π. At each
nontrivial Riemann zero the constant vanishes and the quadratic
coefficient is nonzero. This order-two attachment concerns the reference
with the single zero-frequency mode removed. The unsimplified reference
includes that mode. The two offset directional probes are both regular
at this angle.

4. What the two 30-degree statements mean

A relative clockwise turn of 30 degrees was the prescribed experiment.
At an absolute native angle of ±30 degrees the first trace correction is
(1+2 sin θ)/cos²θ. It measures the first coefficient of the dynamical
determinant minus that of the cosine reference, using −E'(0). At −30
degrees both traces are 4/3. At +30 degrees they are 4 and 4/3. The
common arithmetic reference is π^(−w)(3^w−1)ζ(w); reflection exchanges
the two residue classes. The oriented dynamical term distinguishes them.

reference-zero paths have the following endpoints on the w-scale.

  4πi/log 3  ->  first Riemann zero (4,964 contracting disks)
