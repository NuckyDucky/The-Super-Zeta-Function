Arithmetic reference continuation on the right-hand witness sweep

Write b = 1/2 - theta/pi and
R(w,b) = pi^(-w) (zeta(w,b) + zeta(w,1-b)).
The clockwise subarc from +60 to +45 degrees increases b from 1/6 to 1/4.
All zeros tracked here are zeros of this varying corrected arithmetic reference.

At q=3,4,6, the two reduced residue classes are +1 and -1. At b=1/q,
the sum in R gives (q/pi)^w zeta(w) times the product of 1-p^(-w)
over the distinct prime divisors p of q. Its theta derivative is
w(q/pi)^(w+1)L(w+1,chi_q), with chi_q(+1)=1, chi_q(-1)=-1 and chi_q
vanishing on nonunits. At b=1-1/q the derivative is its negative.
These follow by grouping the Dirichlet series for Re(w)>1, by analytic
continuation, and by the Hurwitz parameter derivative (DLMF 25.11.17).
The derivative has nonzero value at every nontrivial Riemann zero since
the Dirichlet Euler product converges absolutely at w+1 (DLMF 25.15.2).

arithmetic_reference.py runs the local checks at the first Riemann zero,
including a single uniform contraction disk for each angular perturbation
and an independently refined disk at its far endpoint. The precision is
384 bits. The JSON includes the signs of the corresponding angular speeds.

arithmetic_braid.py tracks a zero throughout the full subarc at 224 bits.
For a parameter segment I centered at b0, a provisional center c and a
complex constant D are computed using local Newton steps. Bounds on the
complex derivative on a square containing |w-c| <= r give a bound k on
|1-R_w/D| valid throughout the disk and for every b in I. The segment
passes if k<1 and |R(c,b)/D| + k r < r uniformly in b. The residual bound
uses the center residual and an interval bound on R_b along I. These two
tests make w -> w - R(w,b)/D a self-map and contraction of the closed disk.
Hence there is a unique simple root in that disk for each b in I. At each
boundary a new radius 1e-14 disk is included in the preceding and following
tubes. A residual and derivative check again gives existence and uniqueness
inside this common disk. This joins the roots on consecutive segments.
