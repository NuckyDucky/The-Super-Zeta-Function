ASYMPTOTIC FORM OF THE LEFTWARD ZERO PATHS AT 45 DEGREES

Set b<1/4, a=cos(2πb), h=1−2a², and let b increase to 1/4.
For Re w<0 the Hurwitz Fourier formula factors the bilateral Hurwitz
function into a gamma/trigonometric factor times

    S(w,b) = sum over m≥1 of cos(2πmb) m^(w−1).

The prefactor is nonzero in a neighborhood of each of the nonreal
paths below, including their tails. See https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.11.E9 .
For comparison, Nakamura examines the real branch at the same boundary,
Propositions 4.6–4.7 of https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.10430 .

For each nonzero integer k, write

    w*(a,k) = 1 + log₂(a/h) + 2πik/log 2.

There is a simple zero w_k(b) with

    w_k(b) = w*(a,k) + a/log 2 + O(a^(log₂ 3)).

The first displacement from the two-term solution is therefore real
and independent of k. This is a statement about limiting branches.
Attaching any particular initial condition on the spire to such a
branch requires a separate continuation calculation.

Proof. Put u=w−w*(a,k), fix the circle |u|=1/4, and divide S by a.
The first two summands give 1−2^u exactly. The fourth gives
c₄ (a/h²) 4^u, where c₄=1−8a²+8a⁴. The third and fifth coefficients
are O(a) because their cosine polynomials are odd. After division by a,
their contributions are O(a^(log₂ 3)) and O(a^(log₂ 5)), respectively.
For m≥6 use |cos|≤1 and sum the decreasing powers with the integral
bound starting at m=6. The uniformly bounded factor 6^Re(u) and h→1
give, after division by a, a tail O(a^(log₂ 6−1))=O(a^(log₂ 3)).
These estimates hold on and inside the circle, uniformly in k since the
imaginary powers have unit magnitude.

On the circle, |1−2^u| has a positive minimum. Rouché's theorem now
gives exactly one zero, with multiplicity, in the disk. At this zero,
|1−2^u|=O(a); the quotient (1−2^u)/u stays bounded away from zero on
the closed disk, so u=O(a). Expanding both exponentials in the equation,

    (log 2) u = c₄ a/h² + O(a²) + O(a^(log₂ 3))
              = a + O(a^(log₂ 3)).

The zero is simple and unique in the disk, so the local branches glue
as b varies. In particular Re w tends to −infinity while Im w tends to
2πk/log 2. For k=2 this limiting imaginary coordinate equals the height
of the finite endpoint 4πi/log 2 on a different branch.
