Endpoint-masked forecast on the first ten archived Riemann landings

Question
How well can one local position z, tangent v and acceleration a
predict the terminal complex coordinate? The lead D is measured in
clockwise degrees. Each prediction uses only its own cutoff and
the three formulas z, z+Dv and z+Dv+D²a/2. The last formula allows
both coordinates to move freely. It can overshoot the centerline.

At the last height minimum of the sixth path, the remaining angle
is about 1.590826390814 degrees. At the same time on all ten paths,
the mean complex errors are 0.619830, 0.173423 and 0.083454 for
the position, tangent and quadratic predictions. Their corresponding
median errors are 0.635157, 0.140497 and 0.058007. At that cutoff
each unchanged position already has its own landing as the nearest
known Riemann zero. Endpoint accuracy discriminates the three models.

The quadratic forecast has smaller complex error than the linear
forecast in all 60 paired comparisons. Smooth paths give second-
and third-order local errors for the linear and quadratic forecasts
respectively. The distance over which a chosen accuracy holds is
an empirical property of these particular paths. The six leads
are nested measurements on the same ten previously known cases.

Arrival speed groups at the common trough time
The accelerating five (1,2,5,8,10) have mean and median complex

The two groups have different remaining displacements at the same

Validation and chronology
protocol.json fixes the methods, cutoffs, error thresholds, nearest-
zero baseline and source files. It hashes the forecast code. The data
preparer opens each original archive to extract one past checkpoint
per forecast. The 60 isolated observation processes each receive only
an ordinal, cutoff, cutoff interval and the single eligible point.
Each attaches to the marked root by a contracting Newton disk, uses
interval tubes only up to the cutoff's left endpoint, and bounds the
Hurwitz shift derivatives over the certified cutoff interval. A Python
audit hook bars further file access after each process receives its
packet. For the exact sixth trough, the earlier isolation certificate
supplies both the scalar cutoff interval and the equality Im v=0.

The seal was recorded before scoring. The independent scoring formula
uses FLINT ordinary Riemann zero enclosures. The first seven archived
branches in the atlas were originally traced forward. The last three
were originally traced backward from their known endpoints. Every
branch was included because its endpoint was known from earlier
research. This is a masked local extrapolation on a historical atlas.
The hashes record content and work-session chronology. The experiment
used a local workspace.
