What an arrival measures

Use the marked symmetric Hurwitz sum
    H(w,b) = ζ(w,b) + ζ(w,1−b),
    b = 1/6 + h/180.
The real parameter h counts clockwise degrees from the 60° reference.
The 45° reference lies at h=15 and b=1/4. Write β(z)=L(z,χ₄) for
Dirichlet's beta function and K(w)=4^w(1−2^(−w)). Powers of 2 and
4 use their real logarithms.

1. Velocity and acceleration of a marked zero

At b=1/4 the residue classes 1 and 3 modulo 4 give
    H(w,1/4) = K(w) ζ(w),
    H_b(w,1/4) = −P(w),        P(w) = w 4^(w+1) β(w+1),
    H_bb(w,1/4) = Q(w),        Q(w) = w(w+1)K(w+2)ζ(w+2).
These follow by differentiating the two Hurwitz shifts with their
respective signs. Dirichlet series give the identities in Re w>1.
Analytic continuation gives the meromorphic identities elsewhere.

At a simple H-zero, put v = dw/dh and a = d²w/dh². Differentiating
H(w(h),b(h))=0 once and twice gives, whenever v≠0 at arrival,
    H_w  = P/(180v),
    H_ww = (2P'v/180 − Q/180² − H_w a)/v².
For a simple Riemann zero ρ with K(ρ)≠0, these yield
    ζ'(ρ)  = H_w/K,
    ζ''(ρ) = (H_ww−2K'ζ')/K.
For a simple zero of K with ζ(w)≠0 the equations instead yield
    ζ(w)  = H_w/K',
    ζ'(w) = (H_ww−K''ζ)/(2K').

The enclosed endpoint traces cover the first ten Riemann landings and
one nonzero dyadic lattice endpoint. Every tangent is nonzero. At the
sixth Riemann landing the degree-based tangent and acceleration are
    v = 0.358804723126 + 0.541222727431 i,
    a = −0.346011813121 + 0.143605606255 i.
They give ζ'(ρ₆) = 1.853466249983 − 0.561004420496 i and
ζ''(ρ₆) = −3.203353763732 + 1.373503603108 i. At fixed precision
256 bits, every reconstructed derivative in the archive agrees within
10^(−54) with a direct Riemann Taylor-series evaluation, and the full
interval enclosures overlap. The tests use distinct analytic formulas
with the same FLINT backend. The orbit tubes supply the branch labels.
At the end of the ρ₄ path, K has its simple zero at 8πi/log 2. There
K'=log 2 and the elementary-endpoint formula applies.

2. A replay using two strictly interior positions

Write t=15, u=w(t−ε), z=w(t−2ε), and M=sup |w''| on [t−2ε,t].
The difference of successive increments gives
    |w(t)−(2u−z)| ≤ M ε².
The mean integral of w' between the two interior points gives
    |w'(t)−(u−z)/ε| ≤ 3 M ε/2.
Substituting these enclosures into P/(180Kv) encloses ζ'(ρ₆).
For ε=10^(−8) degrees, the two interior positions alone give
    1.85346625168737 − 0.56100440382440 i
as the central estimate. Its measured error is 1.676×10^(−8).
The endpoint position estimate has certified error below 3.747×10^(−17).
Branch anchoring, the comparison evaluation and uniform derivative bounds
use the certified local family, including the landing for validation.
The interior estimator takes its position and velocity data only at
t−ε and t−2ε. This test reuses a previously located branch.

3. The full arithmetic expansion on the local zero branch

At any marked simple arrival with v≠0, the holomorphic inverse
function theorem makes the shift a local analytic function b(w).
Let d=b(w)−1/4. The same identity holds pointwise at any H(w,b)=0
with |d|<1/4, Re w>0, K(w)≠0 and w≠1. The inverse is only
needed to regard the expansion as a function of w on a neighborhood.
Its exact value is

    K(w) ζ(w)
       = sum over odd j≥1 of (w)_j 4^(w+j) β(w+j) d^j / j!
       − sum over even j≥2 of (w)_j K(w+j) ζ(w+j) d^j / j!.

Here (w)_j=w(w+1)...(w+j−1). Expand H(w,1/4+d) in d and set
the sum to zero. The nearest possible singularity of either shift
to d=0 is d=−1/4, so the expansion converges for |d|<1/4.
The j-th shift derivative is
    (w)_j [ (−1)^j ζ(w+j,1/4) + ζ(w+j,3/4) ].
The two residue classes combine into 4^(w+j)β(w+j) for the odd
terms and K(w+j)ζ(w+j) for the even terms, with the signs displayed
in the exact identity. For j≥1 and Re w>0 each shifted series
on the right converges absolutely. Thus a measured zero branch
and ordinary, convergent arithmetic specify the ζ germ at arrival.
The zero branch itself is obtained from the continued Hurwitz family.

For σ=Re w, q=4|d|, S(s)=(1−2^(−s))ζ(s) and cutoff N≥0,
set r=q max(1,(|w|+N+1)/(N+2)). If r<1, a bound for the modulus
of the tail in ζ(w) is
    S(σ+1) / |1−2^(−w)| · (|w|)_(N+1)/(N+1)! · q^(N+1)/(1−r).
Indeed each residue-class sum after dividing by 4^(w+j) is
bounded by the positive odd-integer sum S(σ+j)≤S(σ+1). The
Pochhammer coefficient has modulus at most (|w|)_j. Successive
terms of this majorant have ratio q(|w|+j)/(j+1), which is ≤r
for j≥N+1. This proves the error bound by a geometric series.

On the sixth-landing path at h=14.5°, eight terms recover
ζ(0.275425623129+37.348848890772i)≈−0.593449543615−0.522635518461i
with analytic tail <9.328×10^(−9). At h=14.9°, eight terms recover
ζ(0.462350158108+37.532865368664i)≈−0.099946296968−0.085286111958i
with analytic tail <3.022×10^(−15). The certified input enclosures
and rounding errors appear separately from the analytic tail in
arrival.json. The measurements use the full input coordinates.

4. Relation to attraction

The complex path has velocity w' and change of speed
Re(conj(w')w'')/|w'|. The ordered exponential orbit has an attracting
multiplier μ. The right flank from 60° to 45° has endpoint moduli
0.204296772848039 and 0.183971328451083. Each of the ten spectral
arrivals is observed at the same final Dottie parameter. Their
speeds range from 0.287279 to 0.649356. Landings 1,2,5,8,10
accelerate and 3,4,6,7,9 slow down at arrival.

On 12πi/log 3 → ρ₆ the speed change at ρ₆ is −0.071498644643.
Reset the very same ρ₆ to h=0 as the seed of the path ending at ρ₈
and its initial speed change becomes +0.076885689946. The two
reference shifts attach different zero germs to that same w.
