Two points on the last turn of the sixth Hurwitz trajectory

Write H(w,b)=zeta(w,b)+zeta(w,1-b), where zeta(w,b) is the Hurwitz zeta
function. The turn angle h is in clockwise degrees, b=1/6+h/180.
At h0=13.4091736091859436753 (the exact stated decimal), p starts at the
simple root near -0.21015253655603836+37.21583036006284i. The second
point starts at q=p+10^-6. Continue p on level zero and q on its own
initial level. Their distinct levels ensure that their distance stays positive.

On each regular disk the complex velocity is v=-H_b/(180 H_w).
Let g=(d/dh) log |q-p|. For z_s=p+s(q-p),

  g  = Re int_0^1 v_w(z_s,b) ds,
  g' = Re int_0^1 [v_wb(z_s,b)/180
                   +v_ww(z_s,b)((1-s)v(p,b)+s v(q,b))] ds.

These equations follow by integrating the complex derivative along the
straight segment from p to q, then differentiating under the integral.
They describe the actual finite gap, for which the two velocities differ.

The 176-bit calculation first encloses the initial root in a disk of radius
2e-31 by a contraction. Propagated disks enclose both solutions at every
angle. The Newton operator with one fixed nonzero complex preconditioner
maps each disk inside itself and is strictly contracting there, uniformly
for both levels and all parameter values in the subinterval. The tiny
endpoint disks overlap the common continuation through strict containment
in that contracting disk. Each working square used to enclose derivatives
contains the entire working disk and hence each interpolating segment.
Taylor enclosures for each Hurwitz derivative use its two first partial
derivatives on the whole working box. That bounds both integrands above.

The directed bounds show g>0 between h0 and 14.24 degrees and g<0 from
14.36 to 15 degrees. Between 14.24 and 14.36 they give g'<0. The signs
of g at 14.298713824150 and 14.298713824153 are opposite, locating its
unique zero. The growing then decreasing distance therefore has one maximum.
The value at the two ends of this small bracket and the upper bound on the
logarithmic slope from its left endpoint enclose the value at the maximum.
On the decreasing part the ratio crosses 1 between 14.879019351554 and
14.879019351557 degrees. The final length is smaller than its launch value
and is the minimum on the closed interval.

Run neighbor.py to reproduce the two numerical meshes. Run neighbor_proof.py
with python-flint and assertions enabled for the directed bounds. The shared
Hurwitz routines are in plane_anchors.py in the atlas directory. Both scripts
accept --output DIRECTORY.
