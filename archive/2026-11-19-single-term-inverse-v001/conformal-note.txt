1. Inverse on a complex spectral zero curve.

Write A(w,b)=ζ(w,b), B(w,b)=ζ(w,1−b) and H=A+B. On the open
complex b-strip 0<Re b<1, these are holomorphic in (w,b) away
from w=1, using the usual Hurwitz continuation. At a zero with
H_w nonzero the holomorphic implicit function theorem gives w(b).
For the marked readout C(b)=A(w(b),b), differentiation yields

    w' = −H_b/H_w,
    C' = (A_b B_w − A_w B_b)/H_w,
    A_b = −w ζ(w+1,b),     B_b = w ζ(w+1,1−b).

The nonvanishing of C' gives a holomorphic local inverse for the
complex reading. With h measured in clockwise degrees along the
chosen real sweep, b=1/6+h/180 and w_h'=w_b'/180. Reversing the
sweep reverses the directed velocity. The complex coordinate
statement concerns the analytic continuation in b, while the
physical experiment travels on a real interval of b.

2. The coordinate folds at the earlier order-contrast threshold.

D(k)=k(k−1)(k−2)/12 is the previously derived fourth-order

There is an explicit collision strictly inside this 15° sweep.
At b=1/5 and b=(13−√73)/20, the position is w=−2 and the labeled

3. The local conformal coordinate on the physical parameter plane.

On the continued right-flank branch set g_L(z)=exp(L exp(−Lz)),
G(L,z)=g_L^320(z), and μ(L)=∂z G(L,p(L)). Both G and its partial
derivatives are entire. At each of the calibrated parameters the
marked periodic point satisfies G(L,p)=p and |μ|<1. Since μ−1 is
nonzero, p(L) is locally holomorphic, and consequently so is μ(L).
The nonzero multiplier has a continuous local logarithm. At
L=x₀+iy the chain rule in radians gives

    d arg μ(x₀+iy)/dy = Im[i μ'(L)/μ(L)] = Re[μ'(L)/μ(L)].

The strictly positive native angle slope on the certified right
flank implies μ'(L)≠0. Thus μ itself is a local conformal coordinate
on the physical L-plane. Inside its local inverse chart consider

    L(r)=μ^(-1)(r exp(iθ₀))

for positive radii r close to the original |μ| and smaller than
one. The corresponding periodic orbits remain attracting and have
the same native phase. Their shift b and all marked Hurwitz data
on the same chosen spectral sheet agree exactly. Their contraction
moduli differ by construction. The vertical-flank restriction
removes this freedom since the chart's phase is strictly monotone
in y there. This explains precisely why a phase-based reading can
identify a point on the calibrated flank and why the physical
parameter off that flank also needs radial information.

In fact one conformal multiplier chart covers an open neighborhood
of the entire closed 60° to 45° right-flank segment K. To glue the
periodic-point germs, choose disks along K small enough that the
implicit solution agrees with the continued p(L) on K in each disk.
Two overlapping disks with centers on this straight segment have
a common subinterval of K. Their germs agree there, so the identity
theorem glues them throughout the lens. A finite subcover gives an
open neighborhood with a single holomorphic p and multiplier μ.
On K, μ' is nonzero by the measured angle slope, and μ is injective
because its continuous argument is strictly monotone over just 15°.
Shrink to a neighborhood where μ' remains nonzero. If every thinner
tube about K contained distinct points L_j, M_j of the same
multiplier, compactness would yield subsequences approaching two
points of K of the same multiplier. Injectivity on K makes these
limit points the same, and local injectivity at that point contradicts
the collision for large j. Thus μ is univalent on some open tube
about K. Its thickness is existential. No global injectivity elsewhere
in the full physical parameter plane is needed for this conclusion.

4. One fold per negative even branch on this same physical sweep.

For each positive integer m the entire slice w=−2m has H=0 and
C_m(b)=−B_(2m+1)(b)/(2m+1). Its fold occurs at the zero b_m of
B_(2m)(b) in (1/6,1/4). For even n=2m the classical zero bounds
for B_n, reflected about 1/2, give

    1/(2^(n+2) π) < 1/4−b_m < 1/(2^(n+1) π),
    1/4−b_m ~ 1/(2^(n+1) π).

These inequalities put the zero strictly inside the sweep even
at n=2. Its simplicity and uniqueness there follow from
B_n'=n B_(n−1): for n>=4 odd Bernoulli polynomials B_(n−1)
have no root in (0,1/2); for n=2, B_1=b−1/2. Hence C_m has
exactly one nondegenerate quadratic fold inside the sweep. In
clockwise degrees these folds approach the 15° endpoint with
deficit asymptotic to (90/π)4^(−m). The Bernoulli zero theorem is
classical. Here it classifies the coordinate failures of the
labeled summand on every negative even slice of the spectral family.
