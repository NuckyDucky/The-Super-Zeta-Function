# Blind trial for an unknown integral lift and chronological word

This protocol fixes the family, the measurements, the matching rule and the expected ambiguity before generating the trial.

1. Take every integer lift index from −5 through 5, and every sign word of lengths zero through five. Read the word from left to right in the order in which its stages act. The public family has 693 labeled candidates. The decoder receives an anonymous packet for each. Its inputs carry no word, length, lift, parity or orientation label.
2. Generate a 256-bit random seed. Order the candidates by SHA-256 with this seed to assign packet IDs. Publish a salted SHA-256 commitment to the entire assignment with the packets. Keep the seed in a separate reveal file. The archived seed and protocol allow exact replay of the assignment.
3. Start with the Taylor series of `1+h`. For each letter σ, replace the series by its exponential after multiplication by σmπi. Compute at 192-bit precision using python-flint 0.9.0 and truncate after degree two. Publish the value, first derivative and twice the quadratic coefficient (the ordinary second derivative), each as a rectangular complex ball. Retain at most 42 significant decimal digits in the displayed intervals, with outward error bounds. Save exact zero as zero.
4. In a separate invocation that reads only the packets, compute the quotient of the second derivative by the first whenever the denominator enclosure excludes zero. An exactly zero denominator has a separate undefined outcome. Any other denominator containing zero makes this test inconclusive.
5. Generate candidate predictions independently from chronological prefix products. For a nonempty word, set s = (−1)^m, t = s mπi and c_j equal to the product of the first j letters. The predictions are value s, first derivative c_n t^n, and quotient s Σ c_j t^j for m ≠ 0. Multiply the quotient by the first derivative to obtain the second derivative. An empty word has jet (1,1,0) for every lift. At zero lift every nonempty word has jet (1,0,0).
6. For every packet and every candidate, record compatibility for the quotient alone and for the three raw readings jointly. Compatibility requires overlapping enclosures in each measured quantity or a shared undefined outcome for the quotient. Record every compatible candidate. An incompatible finite comparison must have disjoint balls in at least one relevant reading. The rule makes no closest-point guesses.
7. Save the complete calls before opening the reveal. Check the commitment, the archived packet values, and both sets of calls against the revealed labels. The predicted ambiguity for a nonempty word w and m ≠ 0 is exactly {(m,w),(−m,−w)}, where −w flips every letter. This includes every all-minus word, its all-plus partner at the opposite lift, and the all-plus candidate at the original lift as a competing observation. The eleven empty-word cases form one group and the sixty-two nonempty zero-lift cases form another. Record failures and any surplus candidate in either channel. Report the ordered comparison counts, where both orders and each self-comparison are included.

The following constructive rule was added before generating any packets. Apply it to the same anonymous quotient, without supplying the candidate list, word length or maximum lift. Classify each Cartesian component by whether its absolute value is strictly below or strictly above 1. For this model, each nonzero component has magnitude greater than 1 by geometric dominance of the leading power of its parity. Two small components indicate the empty word. One large component gives the lift estimate |K|/π. With two large components use the larger absolute component divided by the smaller and then by π. Round only when the whole estimate lies strictly between consecutive half-integers. This gives the proposed positive lift magnitude q.

With r = qπ and M the largest absolute component, find the positive degree n from r^n/2 < M < r^(n+1)/2. Take the base t = (−1)^q qπi and initialize the residual as (−1)^q K. Read each prefix product in order from degree n down to 1 using the sign of the real part of the residual divided by t^j. Subtract the recovered term at each step. Require every sign to be strict and the final residual to contain zero. Recover the chronological word by multiplying adjacent prefix products, starting at c_0 = 1. Record the full positive-lift call and compare it to the sealed assignment after decoding. For each nonzero lift magnitude, report the largest outward upper bound on the absolute rounding error.

The published reveal makes this a replayable computational blind test. The decoder has access to the specified finite candidate family. The proof in the article addresses arbitrary integral lifts and finite lengths.
