# Recovering the conjugate branch from a marked Gaussian spectrum

The primes generated by $3+8i$ and $3-8i$ both have norm 73. Conjugating my quartic exchanges their two signs while preserving the ordinary field zeta. I prove that keeping the positive imaginary direction from my ordered lift distinguishes the two quartic branches, even when the only data supplied are the complex zeros of the completed angular Hecke function. On the exponential side, retaining the first prime in the reading gives two words with different values at the same starting cap.

## The two unknowns and the orientation convention

Write $A=N-D$ for the quadratics in the six-coefficient construction. Use $\delta=-5045155+1027708i$, the discriminant of $A+2iN$, and $\bar\delta$ for the discriminant of $A-2iN$. Write $\eta$ for the quadratic character on Gaussian ideals associated with the chosen branch. The coordinate frame is $z=m+n\tau$, with the ordered ratio $\tau=U/V=i$ taken at native $u=\pi/4$. The two possible names are calibrated relative to this choice of $i$. Reversing that frame together with the branch leaves the labeling ambiguous.

Conjugation is a norm-preserving bijection of ideals and exchanges $\eta_\delta((z))$ with $\eta_{\bar\delta}((\bar z))$. The complete scalar Dedekind zetas of the conjugate fields thus agree. The original determinant $E(v)=\cos^2\sqrt v-v$ is built from the multiplier-one return and is independent of the branch polynomial. The earlier Dottie readings $M_\pm=M(\theta\pm\pi/6)$ detect a return angle from two labeled spectral measurements. Exchanging the polynomial branch while holding the Dottie input fixed leaves these particular readings identical. ## A labeled angular measurement and its inverse

For a nonzero Gaussian ideal $(z)$ use the classical character $\omega_4((z))=(z/|z|)^4$. It is multiplicative and independent of the choice among its four generators because every Gaussian unit has fourth power one. Conjugating the ideal conjugates its reading. For $\Re w>1$ define

$$
 W_\pm(w)= L_{\mathbb Q(i)}(w,\eta\,\omega_4^{\pm1})
 =\frac14\sum_{m+ni\ne0}\eta((m+ni))
 \left(\frac{m+ni}{|m+ni|}\right)^{\pm4}|m+ni|^{-2w}.
$$

This is an angular extension of the project's field-lattice reading. The classical angular factor supplies the orientation; the original quartic supplies $\eta$; the ordered lift supplies the basis. At real $w>1$ the two values are complex conjugates. Either labeled reading suffices once the handedness is fixed. An unordered pair gives exactly the same data on the two branches. [Rudnick and Waxman, *Angles of Gaussian primes*, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07498#page=13)

At a split odd rational prime $p=z\bar z$ fix $z=a+bi$, $\theta=\arg z$. Write $e=\eta((z))$, $f=\eta((\bar z))$. In the norm-only Hecke series and $W_+$ respectively, the coefficients at $p^{-w}$ are

$$
 S_p=e+f,\qquad C_p=(e+f)\cos(4\theta)+i(e-f)\sin(4\theta).
$$

The second measurement restores the missing difference. The exact inverse is $e=(S_p+\Im C_p/\sin4\theta)/2$, $f=(S_p-\Im C_p/\sin4\theta)/2$. For an odd split prime, $ab(a^2-b^2)\ne0$, so $\sin4\theta=4ab(a^2-b^2)/p^2\ne0$. This also handles a zero character value at a conductor prime.

For $p=73$, choose $z=3+8i$. Then $\sin4\theta=-5280/5329$. At the branch $A+2iN$ the coefficient $S_{73}$ is zero and $C_{73}=10560i/5329$. The conjugate branch has the same scalar coefficient and the negative angular coefficient. At 13 both signs are negative, at 37 both are positive, and each of these norm shells has vanishing imaginary part on both branches. For a conjugation-invariant pairing of all measurements the ambiguity returns.

## Whole-series test with the mixed prime 73 withheld

The executable [protocol](protocol.md) specifies two anonymized branches, a known orientation convention and a decoder that sees only full-series enclosures. Norm 17 supplies the independent calibration. The norms through 33 are used as the finite head. The remaining infinite tail includes 73. The branch at 73 is checked afterward by counting the roots of the original polynomial in the two residue fields. This is an identifiability test with a known forward model.

For the plus polynomial branch the first nonreal norm coefficient is at 17. The two marked prime ideals with generators $1+4i,1-4i$ have signs $-,+$, and $\Im C_{17}=480/289$. Direct enumeration of ideals gives zero for every other imaginary coefficient through norm 33. The number of Gaussian ideals of norm $n$ is at most the number of positive divisors of $n$, hence at most $n$ (also from the Euler factors of $\zeta_{\mathbb Q(i)}$). At $w=8$ the uncomputed imaginary tail has absolute value at most $\sum_{n>33} n^{1-8}\le 33^{-6}/6$. Therefore

$$
\left|17^8\Im W_+(8)-480/289\right|\le\frac{17^8}{6\cdot33^6}.
$$

The enclosed scaled value is between $0.760663$ and $2.561136$. The conjugate branch gives the opposite interval. For both anonymous packets the decoder selected the correct polynomial. ## A signed reading of the completed zeros alone

Keep the $+$ orientation and write $\Lambda_\eta$ for the completion of $W_+$. I use the primitive quadratic character of my quartic field at its conductor and the angular character unramified at every finite place. Their product is primitive at that conductor and has nonzero infinity type. In particular it is a nontrivial unitary Hecke character. Its completion is entire of order one. On the real axis to the right of 1 its gamma factor is a positive real multiple of an exponential times $\Gamma(w+2)$ and has real logarithmic second derivative. The conductor contributes a real linear function to the logarithm. Therefore, by twice differentiating the genus-one Hadamard product, the following is an absolutely convergent statistic of the completed zeros, including multiplicity:

$$
 Z_\eta(x)=-\Im\sum_{\Lambda_\eta(\rho)=0}\frac{1}{(x-\rho)^2}
          =\Im(\log W_+)''(x),\qquad x>1 .
$$

To see which part of the completion I can read from its zeros, write one genus-one factor as $(1-w/\rho)e^{w/\rho}$. Its logarithmic derivative is $(w-\rho)^{-1}+\rho^{-1}$. One more derivative gives $-(w-\rho)^{-2}$. The exponential prefactor $e^{a+bw}$ contributes zero to this derivative, and a zero of order $m$ at the origin contributes $-m/w^2$. For an entire function of order one, the sum of $|\rho|^{-2}$ over nonzero zeros converges. On a compact set away from the zeros, all sufficiently distant terms satisfy $|w-\rho|^{-2}\leq4|\rho|^{-2}$. This permits differentiation of the logarithmic derivative term by term. At real $x>1$ the Euler product also guarantees $W_+(x)\ne0$. The gamma factor contributes a real number there, leaving the displayed identity on taking imaginary parts. [Lemke Oliver and Thorner, §2, completion and order](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.03122#page=10) · [Rudnick and Waxman, §4.1, Gaussian angular gamma factor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07498#page=13)

I evaluated the right side using the absolutely convergent differentiated Euler series. A prime ideal of norm $q$ contributes $j(\log q)^2(\eta\omega_4)(\mathfrak p)^j/q^{jx}$ at its $j$th power. Below norm 34 only norm 17 contributes an imaginary part. Above that, at norm $n=q^j$, $j(\log q)^2\le(\log n)^2\le n$ and the number of prime-power ideals is at most $n$. Consequently the tail at $x=16$ has magnitude at most $33^{-13}/13$. For the plus polynomial branch,

$$
\left|17^{16}Z_\eta(16)-\frac{480}{289}(\log17)^2\right|
\le\frac{17^{16}}{13\cdot33^{13}}<0.07.
$$

The outward-rounded interval is $[13.264196,13.400212]$ after scaling. The conjugate branch gives its negative. The verifier uses rational interval bounds for $\log17=4\log2+\log(17/16)$. The input to the branch decision is the interval alone. This proves that the two labeled angular completions have different complex zero multisets, counting multiplicity. I evaluated the Euler side of the identity to determine the sign. Reflecting the signed heights exchanges the two multisets, so erasing the signs of the heights loses this distinction. The proof applies for arbitrary real parts of the zeros. The critical-line conjecture relevant to these angular twists is the one for unitary Hecke $L$-functions.

More explicitly, conjugating the summation ideal in the half-plane of absolute convergence gives $W_{+,\bar\delta}(w)=\overline{W_{+,\delta}(\bar w)}$. The completions have the same conductor norm and gamma factor, so continuation carries the identity to their zeros with multiplicity. The two inequalities above therefore distinguish a zero multiset from its reflection across the real axis. The ordinate of the first individual zero where the multiplicities differ remains open.

A zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ contributes $-2(2-\beta)\gamma/((2-\beta)^2+\gamma^2)^2$ to this last statistic. Its sign reverses when $\gamma$ is reflected, so differences of signed heights can be measured even if every zero has real part one half.

Each finite product is a product of linear factors with an affine exponential prefactor. At an evaluation point away from all listed zeros where the limit is nonzero, Lean derives convergence of the first two derivatives and proves the unordered sum identity, counting repeated indices. It also checks the change of sign under complex conjugation at a real input. The Hecke completion, its genus-one factorization and the real gamma contribution for this particular character use the cited analytic theory. ## From prime signs to phrases

Fix an order on the prime ideals being read and use unramified signs as letters for the original exponential maps $E_\pm(x)=2^{\pm x}$, i.e. take $L=\log2$. At 73 read $3+8i$ first. The plus polynomial gives the word $-+$ and sends the marked cap through $1,1/2,\sqrt2$. The minus polynomial gives $+-$ and sends it through $1,2,1/4$. The last sign of a nonempty word is read from the sign of $\log x$; the predecessor is $|\log_2 x|$. The existing Lean theorem proves injectivity of this evaluation for all finite words at positive real lift. The new choice here sends arithmetic signs, in a specified order, to its letters. Multiplication of ideals commutes, so that order must be retained when one asks for a phrase. [Lean word theorem](https://github.com/NuckyDucky/The-Super-Zeta-Function/blob/quartic-field-v001/lean/SuperZeta/SignWords.lean#L251)

**Arbitrary phrases.** For a rooted sequence of unramified marked split primes, apply the local two-reading inverse at each prime in order. Two such sequences have equal outputs at the cap for a fixed real lift $L>0$ if and only if their sign words agree letter for letter. One direction is evaluation of identical words. The other is the injectivity theorem. Different primes with the same letter can give the same encoded input, so identifying the primes also requires their labels. The two-letter encoding takes weights $+1$ and $-1$. At a ramified input a zero weight requires a third symbol if that input is to be included.

Lean 4.19 checks the local inverse and both signs using the original quadratic's root counts at 73. The theorem `capValues73` evaluates both words exactly. For a finite ordered list of unramified readings, `shell_cap_roundtrip` recovers the signs in order, applies those stages to the cap, and reads the signs back from the endpoint. The decoder uses the positive real lift and any supplied upper bound on the word length. At each step it divides the absolute value of the real logarithm by the lift, and stops when the value returns to 1. [Local arithmetic and round trip](../../lean/SuperZeta/MarkedBranch.lean) · [Finite decoder](../../lean/SuperZeta/SignWords.lean)

There is a useful exact control even inside the dynamics. Put $g=B\circ A$ and $h=A\circ B$, where $A,B$ are the two exponential stages. The map $A$ takes the fixed points of $g^n$ bijectively to those of $h^n$; on those points its inverse is $g^{n-1}\circ B$. Since $A\circ g^n=h^n\circ A$ and $A'$ never vanishes, matching periodic points have the same multiplier and primitive period. Thus the two cyclically rotated words at 73 have identical periodic multiplier data. It is the common starting cap that lets the ordered exponential evaluation distinguish their rooted histories.
