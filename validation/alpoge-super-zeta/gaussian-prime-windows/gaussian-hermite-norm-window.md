# A Hermite polynomial for the dyadic prime window

I take $m=\lceil x/2\rceil$ and build one integer polynomial for the whole interval $x/2\le p<x$. If I compare its values with the identity and central-involution readings, the ideal norms give exactly the signed prime sum from the quartic cutoff. [Lean norm identity and field theorem](ArchivedGaussianHermiteNorm.lean) · [Source replay and axioms](gaussian-hermite-norm-audit.txt)

## One polynomial for the interval

Let $D_m=((m-1)!)^2$. I prescribe $W_m(j)=D_mj$ and $W_m'(j)=0$ at $j=0,\ldots,m-1$. The integer formula is

$$
W_m(X)=D_mX-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{j}^{\!2}(X-j)\prod_{\substack{0\le i<m\\i\ne j}}(X-i)^2.
$$

If $m\le p<2m$ is prime, then $D_m X^p$ has those same values and zero derivatives modulo $p$. The difference has degree below $2m$ and a double root at every one of the $m$ distinct nodes. It follows that $W_m(X)\equiv D_mX^p\pmod p$. Lean also checks $X^2\mid W_m$ from the conditions at the node $0$. [Lean polynomial and congruence](GaussianHermitePrimePolynomial.lean)

## One power at a matching prime

For an automorphism $g$, form the ideal generated by $W_m(a)-D_m g(a)$ over the integral nodes $a$ of the splitting field $L$. If an unramified prime ideal $P$ over $p$ divides this ideal, the congruence and $p\nmid D_m$ say that the reduction of $g$ is arithmetic Frobenius at $P$. Conversely, if the reductions agree, every generator lies in $P$. At the specific node $a=p$, $W_m(p)\in P^2$ and $D_mg(p)=D_mp\notin P^2$. The exponent at $P$ is therefore one on a match and zero otherwise. [Lean ideal valuation](GaussianHermiteFrobeniusIdeal.lean)

I keep the factors over $x/2\le p<x$ and write the resulting ideal as $I_g(x)$. Above the fixed discriminant cutoff, if $g$ is the identity $1$ or central involution $z$, every prime ideal over a matching $p$ has exponent one. Their product is $(p)$, with norm $p^8$ in the degree-eight field $L$. This gives

$$
\log N I_g(x)=8C_g(x),\qquad
\frac{\log N I_z(x)-\log N I_1(x)}{4}
=2(C_z(x)-C_1(x))=D(x).
$$

Here $D(x)$ is the original signed dyadic sum from the quartic. The last equality holds once the cutoff has also passed the conductor exceptions. If the left-hand side has the upper bound $A_\theta x^\theta$ throughout arbitrarily late windows $[Y,Y^{1+\delta_\theta}]$ for each $1/2<\theta<1$, Lean gives Dedekind RH for the quartic with the rational Dirichlet input. The upper bound is the remaining hypothesis. [Exact cutoff and Lean premises](ArchivedGaussianHermiteNorm.lean) · [Cofinal window definition](GaussianRawWindowCriterion.lean#L189)

## A Gaussian integer form of the central count

The following is a written derivation. If $m=2N+1$, reflecting the prescribed nodes gives $W_m(m-1-X)=D_m(m-1)-W_m(X)$ by the same interpolation uniqueness. Thus $W_m(N+T)-D_mN=T A_m(T^2)$ for an integer polynomial $A_m$. The congruence for $W_m$ gives $A_m(S)\equiv D_m S^{(p-1)/2}\pmod p$ at every odd prime in $[m,2m)$. Choosing the least odd $m\ge x/2$ still includes every odd prime of the dyadic interval.

Take $\delta=-5045155+1027708i$ from the archived quartic. At a split prime, $A_m(\delta)\equiv-D_m$ as a Gaussian integer modulo $p$ gives the negative Euler sign at both marked primes. This is the central reading. At an inert prime the same congruence would imply $\delta^p=\delta$, since $\delta^{(p-1)/2}=-1$. Inert Frobenius conjugates $\delta$, so this can happen only at primes dividing $2\operatorname{Im}\delta$. Once the interval has passed the finite exceptions, if $P_x=\prod_{x/2\le p<x}p$, the result is

$$
C_z(x)=\log\gcd\bigl(P_x,\operatorname{Re}(A_m(\delta)+D_m),\operatorname{Im} A_m(\delta)\bigr).
$$

For $m=7$, $D_7=518400$ and $A_7(S)=2018016S-2426424S^2+1154868S^3-252252S^4+25116S^5-924S^6$. At $13$, the two reductions are $A_7(\delta)\equiv1$ and $D_7\equiv12$, giving the central sign. At $11$, the same test would give the negative sign even though $11$ is inert in the base field. It divides $1027708$, so the exception matters at that level. ## Size forced by the congruences

If an integer polynomial $P$ reduces to $cX^{d(p)}$ at every prime $p$ in a set $S$, with distinct degrees $d(p)$ and $p\nmid c$, then its coefficient at $d(p)$ is nonzero and divisible by every other prime in the set. Lean checks the joint bound

$$
\left|[X^{d(p)}]P\right|\ \ge\ \prod_{q\in S\setminus\{p\}} q.
$$

This applies both to the original Hermite polynomial and to the distinct monomial degrees $(p-1)/2$ in the written centered form. For $m=7$ and $p=13$, the leading coefficient $-924$ is divisible by $7\cdot11=77$.

If I restrict $S$ to the two rationally identified Frobenius classes, the log of the prime product is $x/8+O(\sqrt x\log^2 x)$ before dropping $p$, using the written Dirichlet-GRH estimate for the combined classes. The target upper bound on the central class alone is $x/16+O_\theta(x^\theta)$. A common coefficient-height bound still has to accommodate the larger product. A bound using the value at the actual Gaussian radicand would need to control cancellations or discarded prime factors.
