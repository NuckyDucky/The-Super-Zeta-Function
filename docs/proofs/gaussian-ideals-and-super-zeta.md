# The Gaussian ideals of the quartic and the corrected super zeta

My original quadratic gives two Gaussian factors of a rational quartic. I choose the factor with discriminant $\delta=-5045155+1027708i$, put $k=\mathbb Q(i)$ and write $E=k(\sqrt{\delta})$. My ordered logarithms supply the marked square lattice $U/V=i$. Independently, the multiplier-one spectrum of that tower recovers the ordinary zeta factor in $\zeta_E$. The quartic supplies the further Hecke factor $G(w)=L_k(w,\eta_\delta)$. Here I compute their contributions to the same field and isolate which prime signs could detect an off-line Gaussian zero. ## Two ideals of norm 73 and one prime ideal of norm 5329

Write $\theta=\sqrt\delta$. In the field generated by the original quartic root I recover $i$ and $\theta$. The integral minimal polynomial of $\theta$ is $Y^4+10090310Y^2+26509772707289$. The prime 73 satisfies the good-reduction condition for this generator. The reduced polynomial is

$$
(Y-9)(Y-64)(Y^2-34)\pmod {73}.
$$

| Ideal in $\mathbb Z[i]$ | Residue of $i$ | Residue of $\delta$ | Factorization above it in $E$ |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| $(3+8i)$ | $27$ | $34$, a nonsquare | One prime of norm $73^2$ |
| $(3-8i)$ | $46$ | $8=9^2=64^2\bmod73$ | Two primes of norm $73$, distinguished by $\theta=9$ or $64$ in their residue fields |

The two ideals of norm 73 therefore both lie above $(3-8i)$. In mathlib’s ideal-counting definition of the Dedekind zeta, the absolute norm of an ideal is the cardinality of its quotient ring. Lean proves that exactly two ideals in the actual ring of integers have this norm. It derives the minimal polynomial and good reduction from the field model. [Original quartic root generates the field](../../lean-ideal73/OriginalQuarticBranch.lean) · [Unconditional ideal count](../../lean-ideal73/QuarticAt73.lean) · [All three prime ideals, their norms and their Gaussian parent](../../lean-ideal73/PrimeDecomposition73.lean)

The signed **Gaussian** coefficient at 73 is zero, since the signs add to $-1+1$. The **field** coefficient at 73 is two. With $T=73^{-w}$ their local factors are $1/(1-T^2)$ and $1/[(1-T)^2(1-T^2)]$, respectively. By unique factorization of nonzero ideals, every field ideal of norm $73^n$ has exponents $a,b,c\ge0$ at the three primes, with $a+b+2c=n$. Each choice of $c$ gives $n-2c+1$ choices for $a,b$. Thus the field coefficient is $(r+1)^2$ for $n=2r$ and $(r+1)(r+2)$ for $n=2r+1$. Lean constructs this bijection in the ring of integers of $E$ and proves both formulas for the actual field ideals. In particular the first five field coefficients, starting at norm $1$, are $1,2,4,6,9$. ## What the tower contributes at a zero of the field

Put $D(s)=\mathcal Z(s;1)-J(s;1)$ and $A(s)=2\pi^{-2s}(2^{2s}-1)$. The multiplier-one construction gives $D(s)=A(s)\zeta(2s)$. The quartic field has $\zeta_E(w)=\zeta(w)\beta_D(w)G(w)$, where $\beta_D=L(\cdot,\chi_{-4})$ is Dirichlet beta. Combining these equalities gives, with $w=2s$,

$$
D(s)\,\beta_D(2s)\,G(2s)=A(s)\,\zeta_E(2s).
$$

Since $A$ is nonzero for $0<\Re s<1/2$, the order of a zero of $\zeta_E$ at $w$ is the sum of the orders of $D$ at $w/2$ and of $\beta_D,G$ at $w$. This holds even when two or three factors vanish at once. Lean takes the analyticity of the field and Hecke functions away from 1 and their arithmetic factorization on $\Re w>1$ as inputs. It continues the identity and proves the order statement using the repository’s corrected spectrum and mathlib’s Dirichlet beta. [Arithmetic derivation](../../archive/2026-11-20-dedekind-phase-v001/README.md) · [Formal hypotheses, continuation and orders](../../lean/SuperZeta/GaussianSpectralTransfer.lean)

At a simple zero $s_0$ of $D$ in that strip with $\beta_D(2s_0)\ne0$, differentiation gives an additional reading at the zero itself,

$$
G(2s_0)=\frac{2A(s_0)\,\zeta_E'(2s_0)}{D'(s_0)\,\beta_D(2s_0)}.
$$

The factor 2 comes from differentiating $2s$. At the first positive Riemann zero, the existing interval calculation separates $\zeta'$ and $\beta_D$ from zero. An independent reading of $\zeta_E'$ there would consequently determine $G$, including whether it vanishes. ## An independent check at 65,536 ideal norms and at $w=4$

I enumerated Gaussian ideals using the unique generator $a+bi$ with $a>0$, $b\ge0$. For each norm $n$ I counted the ideals, then summed their quartic character signs separately. Their Dirichlet convolution agreed at **every norm from 1 through 65,536** with PARI’s ideal counts computed from the quartic minimal polynomial. This includes signed coefficient 1 and field coefficient 4 at $73^2$.

At $w=4$ the determinant gives $\mathcal Z(2;1)=10/3$. To evaluate $J(2;1)$, the coefficient of $t^2$ in $\log(1+t/\cosh^2\sqrt t)$ is $-3/2$. Its term in the continued Mellin integral is $(-3/2)/(2-s)$, multiplied by $s\sin(\pi s)/\pi$. Taking the limit gives $J(2;1)=3$. Thus the corrected trace entering this comparison is exactly $1/3$.

| Calculation of $\zeta_E(4)$ | Enclosure, rounded outward |
|---|---|
| Sum over quartic ideal norms from PARI, including its tail bound | $1.1451211344264321\ \pm 1.87\times10^{-9}$ |
| Corrected quartet trace, Dirichlet beta and signed Gaussian ideal series | $1.1451211325637895\ \pm 9.97\times10^{-10}$ |

For $n>N$, $n^{-4}\le N^{-2}n^{-2}$. At each rational prime the coefficients counting ideals of a degree-$d$ field are bounded by those of $\zeta^d$, and $\zeta(2)<2$. This gives a one-sided tail of at most $16/N^2$ for the quartic and a two-sided tail bound of $4/N^2$ for the signed Gaussian sum. Dirichlet beta has absolute tail at most $1/(3N^3)$. The replay checks that the two enclosures overlap using 160-bit interval arithmetic. ## Which prime signs detect an off-line Gaussian zero?

Exclude the finite set of rational primes dividing $2N_{k/\mathbb Q}(\delta)$. Among the remaining split primes, let $\mathcal S$ be those where the two quartic signs agree, and call their common sign $\epsilon_p$. For $\Re w>1$ define

$$
P(w)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal S}\epsilon_p p^{-w},
\qquad S(w)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal S}\epsilon_p(\log p)p^{-w}.
$$

**Theorem.** $S$ has a meromorphic continuation to $\Re w>1/2$. Its poles there are exactly the zeros of $G$. A zero of multiplicity $m$ gives a simple pole of residue $-m/2$.

**Proof.** Write $\log G=2P+H$ using the Euler-product logarithm on $\Re w>1$. Put $u=p^{-w}$. Equal signs give $-2\log(1-\epsilon_p u)$, whose first term is $2\epsilon_p u$. Opposite signs give $-\log(1-u^2)$. Inert rational primes already have Gaussian norm $p^2$, so their logarithms also start in degree two. The finitely many excluded primes contribute local logarithms analytic for $\Re w>0$. After removing $2P$, the remaining logarithmic series and its derivative converge uniformly on each compact subset of $\Re w>1/2$, by comparison with $\sum_{n\ge2}(1+\log n)n^{-2\sigma}$ for a fixed $\sigma>1/2$. This defines $H$ throughout that half-plane. Differentiation gives

$$
S(w)=\tfrac12\left(H'(w)-\frac{G'(w)}{G(w)}\right).
$$

The primitive nontrivial Hecke function $G$ is holomorphic there. Near a zero $\rho$ write $G(w)=(w-\rho)^m h(w)$ with $h(\rho)\ne0$. The only singular term of $S$ is $-m/[2(w-\rho)]$. This calculation continues the prime sum as a function. Convergence of the original prime series below $\Re w=1$ is a separate question. [Quartic conductor and character](../../archive/2026-11-20-dedekind-phase-v001/README.md#the-conductor-and-the-sign-at-every-gaussian-integer) · [Opposite-sign product and Dirichlet identity](../../archive/2026-11-20-gaussian-local-factor-v001/README.md)

The entire closed right Dottie disk lies in this half-plane, with leftmost real coordinate $1025/2048$. For its positively oriented circumference $C$, provided $G$ is nonzero on $C$, the number of Gaussian zeros inside is $-(\pi i)^{-1}\int_C S(w)\,dw$, counting multiplicity. A holomorphic extension of this same $S$ across the entire disk would clear it. The completed reflection would clear its mirror. The opposite signs at 73, and at every other prime of that type, contribute to $H$ and cannot supply a pole in this region. [Circle geometry and reflection](../../archive/2026-11-20-gaussian-circle-phases-v001/README.md) · [Argument principle](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.10.E9)

Equivalently, a holomorphic continuation of the equal-sign series $S$ throughout $\Re w>1/2$ would give zero freedom for $G$ there. This implication can be established while a hypothetical zero is still allowed. Continue the denominator-free identity $2SG=H'G-G'$ from $\Re w>1$, then apply the local residue formula at that zero. One sufficient arithmetic estimate, by partial summation, would be $\sum_{p\in\mathcal S,\ p\le x}\epsilon_p\log p=O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. The finite counts above do not give that all-scale estimate. [Lean proofs of the residue and the connected-domain criterion](../../lean/SuperZeta/GaussianPrimeResidue.lean)

### Crossley’s compact dyadic filter

The [Gaussian prime-window calculation](../../archive/2026-11-21-quartic-projections-v001/windows/README.md) defines the signed ramp and the first dyadic difference. Lee Crossley contributed the following connection in [PR #50](https://github.com/NuckyDucky/The-Super-Zeta-Function/pull/50).

Write the original sum as $R$, so $H_2(X)=2R(X/2)-R(X)$. Then $K(X)=H_2(X)-H_2(X/2)=-R(X)+3R(X/2)-2R(X/4)$ uses only norms $X/4<n<X$. Its Mellin transform gains the factor $1-2^{-s}$, which preserves the poles in $0<\Re s<1$ and the residue signs at real poles. Crossley uses this filter for rational Dirichlet families, with the window bound on the resulting difference. [Lean proof](https://github.com/leecrossley/dirichlet-window-transfer/blob/fa94718cf9d65ee5b143ded4c91dc3873222cfdf/DelayedPole.lean#L114) and [archived result](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24410863).
