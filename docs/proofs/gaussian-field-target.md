# From the Gaussian factor to the quartic field

I use the field $E=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-5045155+1027708i})$ defined by my original quartic. Write $G(s)=L_{\mathbb Q(i)}(s,\eta_\delta)$ for its Gaussian Hecke factor and $\beta_D=L(s,\chi_{-4})$. The proposed field conclusion says that every zero of $\zeta_E$ with $0<\Re s<1$ has $\Re s=1/2$. The field transfer and the horizontal derivative test answer different parts of this question.

### Which function represents the ideal-counting series?

Formal Conjectures quantifies over every function holomorphic away from $1$ that agrees with mathlib’s ideal-counting series on $\Re s>1$. Two such functions agree throughout $\mathbb C\setminus\{1\}$ by the identity theorem. I use these exact definitions at the pinned upstream revision. [Formal Conjectures, definitions at lines 57–64](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/bcb1cdc179c8868c3a7ca651f617c433dcd5102e/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/ArtinPrimitiveRootsConjecture.lean#L57-L64) · [Continuation proof](../../lean-ideal73/FieldTarget.lean)

For the product $\zeta(s)\beta_D(s)G(s)$, Lean proves the following implication for the constructed field. Assume the product is holomorphic off $1$, the field ideal counts and Gaussian coefficients are multiplicative, the Gaussian series sums to $G$ on $\Re s>1$, and the coefficient at **every rational prime power** agrees with the field’s ideal count. Then the product is a continuation of that ideal-counting series. In the strip a product of three complex values vanishes precisely when at least one factor vanishes. Consequently, given the critical-line results for $\zeta$ and $\beta_D$, the field conclusion is **equivalent** to the critical-line result for $G$. The theorem takes those two results explicitly. [Specialization to the quartic, `quarticRH_iff_gaussian`](../../lean-ideal73/FieldCoefficientTransfer.lean)

The field calculation at 73 supplies the ideal counts $(r+1)^2$ at $73^{2r}$ and $(r+1)(r+2)$ at $73^{2r+1}$. The independent calculation through norm 65,536 checks more prime powers over a finite range. The all-prime-power comparison above is the hypothesis these calculations address. ### What do the two Gaussian zeros give the field?

At the conjugate inputs $w_\pm=1/2\pm it_0$ with $0.25835<t_0<0.25838$, the completed Gaussian factor vanishes. Its scale and gamma factors are nonzero there, so $G(w_\pm)=0$. Once the three-factor product satisfies the ideal-series hypotheses above, every admissible continuation $Z$ satisfies $Z(w_+)=Z(w_-)=0$. This deduction uses the product at two specified points and requires no assumption about the location of its other zeros.

The original super zeta $\Phi(w)=\mathcal Z(w/2;1)$ separates the two inputs by more than $0.94434$ in its output plane. If $H$ recovered these readings from the field value, then both $\Phi(w_+)$ and $\Phi(w_-)$ would equal $H(0)$. Lean gives this contradiction for **every** $Z$ satisfying the Formal Conjectures definition, under the field-identification hypotheses above. The spectral and field proofs run in separately pinned packages. The two zero measurements and the distinct readings appear explicitly at their interface. ### Which rectangle would contain only critical-line zeros?

For the local calculation I use the reflected completion $F$, which is real on $\Re s=1/2$. At the heights near $T_D$ the completion multiplies $G$ by a holomorphic nonvanishing factor, so they have the same zeros with the same multiplicities there. If $\Im F'(s)<0$ throughout a rectangle crossing the critical line, then $\Im F$ decreases strictly with the real coordinate at each fixed height. It vanishes at the line, so it has opposite signs on the two open sides. On the line, $\partial_t\Re F=-\Im F'>0$. Opposite signs for the real value at the bottom and top therefore give exactly one zero, with nonzero derivative. [Rectangle and simple-zero theorems](../../lean-ideal73/DerivativeRectangle.lean)

Writing $F=P+R$ separates a measured prefix $P$ from the remainder $R$. If $\Im P'<-m$ throughout the rectangle, any off-line zero forces $\Im R'>m$ somewhere on the horizontal segment from that zero to the line. This is the sign of the remainder that the computation must bound. To transfer a local exclusion to the entire field statement, a second Lean proof exhausts the open right half of the strip by compact rectangles. A positive lower bound for $|G|$ on each such rectangle, together with reflection of zeros, would cover every height. The lower bound may depend on the rectangle.
