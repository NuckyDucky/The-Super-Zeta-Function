## Which factor enters the comparison?

My Gaussian quadratic has discriminant $\delta=-5045155+1027708i$. Put $k=\mathbb Q(i)$ and $K=k(\sqrt\delta)$. The Dedekind zeta of $K$ factors as

~~~math
\zeta_K(w)=\zeta(w)\,\beta_D(w)\,L_k(w,\eta_\delta).
~~~

Here $\beta_D$ is Dirichlet beta. At an unramified prime ideal of $k$, the quadratic character $\eta_\delta$ takes the value $+1$ if that ideal splits in $K$ and $-1$ if it stays prime. Masullo’s calculation in §1.4.1 takes a number field and a Hecke character as inputs. Taking $k$ and $\eta_\delta$ selects the third factor. My tower determinant and its Mellin correction enter the first factor through the substitution $w=2s$. ## What survives at norm 73?

For the branch $A+2iN$, at the two Gaussian primes generated by $z=3+8i$ and $\bar z=3-8i$, my branch gives signs $-1,+1$ in that order. In the ordinary Hecke series their contributions to the coefficient of $73^{-w}$ sum to zero. The same cancellation occurs if the signs are exchanged. To keep the order, I can select a second character in the same framework. On a Gaussian ideal generated by $z$, put $\omega_4((z))=(z/|z|)^4$. All four Gaussian units have fourth power one, so the choice of generator leaves this character unchanged.

In $L_k(w,\eta_\delta\omega_4)$, the coefficient at 73 is

~~~math
-\frac{(3+8i)^4}{73^2}+\frac{(3-8i)^4}{73^2}
=\frac{10560}{5329}\,i.
~~~

Exchanging the branch signs negates this value. The untwisted function is the factor in $\zeta_K$. The angular function is a second measurement that distinguishes the two primes. ### A check on the uniform bound on page 29

In §1.4.2 take the rational field, its trivial character, a real parameter $z>1$, and a nonnegative mollifier. The proposed smooth radial tests vanish near $t=1$ and tend pointwise to $t^{-z}$ for $t>1$. In the positive real component of the Archimedean integral in §1.4.3, the denominator is $t-1$. For fixed $b>1$ the limiting contribution from $1<t<b$, up to a positive constant, is

~~~math
\int_1^b \frac{t^{-z-1/2}}{t-1}\,dt=+\infty.
~~~

The numerator tends to one at the lower endpoint. Fatou’s lemma therefore rules out a uniform upper bound for these individual nonnegative cutoff integrals, although each cutoff integral is finite. An argument that subtracts this logarithmic term or cancels it against another contribution would have to control the combined limit. This calculation tests the separate bound used in the introductory sketch.
